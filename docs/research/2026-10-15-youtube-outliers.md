## 4. Packaging rules learned

- Primary hook = recognized object or place + unexpected situation + clearly defensible stakes.
- Find a **single visual question** before writing the full script. Thumbnail should *add* a clue not repeat the title.
- Objects are first-class thumbnail subjects: poker cards, check, key, ATM, flash drive, lottery ticket.
- Bright, clear high-contrast thumbnails are allowed and often better suited to mobile; there is no rule to use dark documentary imagery everywhere.
- Do not copy an existing thumbnail, transcript, narration or source visuals. The factual case is public and independently researchable, but packaging and telling should be original.
- Avoid synthetic fabricated evidence (fake documents, chats, newsroom screenshots) presented as authentic.
- Source records and date-stamped competitor figures separately; later analytics must distinguish CTR, reach, average view duration and retention. High raw views from million-subscriber channels are not sufficient evidence.
- Reject outlier data that is not an actual documentary case (audiobooks, short local news, compilations, fiction, low-trust clickbait).

## 5. Efficient vidIQ policy

- Do **not** rerun the same queries until the current cache is reviewed. Query usage costs credits.
- Use official YouTube API for your own channel statistics wherever possible, with OAuth and access controls.
- Use vidIQ sparingly for **channel-normalized outliers, specific competitor discovery, and packaging checks**, recording date and URL here.
- Don't use vidIQ's AI video watch at 25 credits unless there's a specific visual question worth paying for; still images and titles can often be checked through the normal public URL.
- Save the conclusions as `docs/research/` Markdown, linked from `AGENTS.md` if needed. Change historical findings rather than silently overwriting snapshots.

## 6. Workflow for future episodes

1. Read this snapshot; form 5–8 potential topics.
2. Verify the primary case in reporting/official records (dates, people, mechanisms, amounts, legal outcomes).
3. Validate title/thumbnail hypotheses and current saturation using public YouTube + fresh vidIQ queries only if needed.
4. Select case by quality of click reason and availability of reliable sources.
5. Write `docs/episodes/<slug>-production.md` with packaging/fact map/visual plan; then `content/longform/<slug>.txt` & `.json`.
6. Preview audio/visual in Kitok with `--renderer native --dry-run`, then run once after permission and backend verification.
7. Measure title/thumbnail and first-minute retention on the published video. Update lessons, not just raw view totals.
